# Indus script: primary-source map and computational constraints

**Status:** evidence map for an attempted decipherment program, **not a decipherment**.  
**Checked:** 2026-08-09 (America/Toronto).  
**Evidence labels:** **Sourced** means directly reported or exposed by the linked source; **Inference** means the conclusion drawn here from multiple sources.

## Bottom line

**Sourced.** The surviving corpus is highly structured: signs have asymmetric initial/final distributions, strong local ordering constraints, repeatable clusters, and geographic/artefact associations. Most intended readings are treated as right-to-left, but seal matrices, impressions, mirrored signs, multiline texts, boustrophedon and rare opposite-direction texts require artifact-level handling. The central obstacles remain exceptionally short texts, no bilingual, no secure phonetic anchor or proper-name identification, unknown language(s), uncertain sign/allograph boundaries, duplicate texts, damaged readings, and uneven archaeological sampling ([Yadav et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0009506); [Kenoyer & Meadow 2010](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/KenoyerMeadow%202010%20Inscribed%20Objects%20from%20Harappa.pdf); [ICIT documentation](https://www.epigraphica.de/indus/help_onlinedatabase.pdf)).

**Inference.** A serious project can already recover *structure*—direction, formula slots, sign classes, allographs, regional/chronological variants, and perhaps numerical or administrative subsystems. It cannot responsibly output English translations from the present evidence. Any semantic reading should be treated as a hypothesis generator until it makes risky, preregistered predictions on held-out inscriptions and survives comparison with non-linguistic codes.

### Sign concordance is a model, not clerical cleanup

**Sourced.** M77 uses 417 signs, Parpola's inventory is commonly smaller, and Wells/ICIT lists 676. The 2021 study found at least 23 mirrored asymmetric pairs and proposed 50 Wells codes as redundant through mirroring, crowding and contextual variation ([Daggumati & Revesz 2021](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-021-00713-0)). ICIT itself separates graphic descriptions from claimed meanings and provides duplicate/TAB-effect controls ([documentation](https://www.epigraphica.de/indus/help_onlinedatabase.pdf)).

**Inference.** Maintain two layers: (1) a conservative *grapheme observation* preserving every visible distinction and uncertainty; (2) versioned *allograph hypotheses* that merge forms for particular experiments. Analyses run only on a prematurely collapsed sign list can manufacture or erase grammatical-looking patterns.

## 2. Archaeological provenance is part of the text

**Sourced.** HARP's 1986–2007 Harappa catalogue links objects to unique excavation units, stratigraphy, spatial coordinates, recovery procedures and museum registrations. Its chronology distinguishes Ravi (ca. 3700–2800 BCE), Kot Diji/Early Harappa (ca. 2800–2600), Mature Harappa 3A–C (ca. 2600–1900), and later phases. The excavators document changes through time in seal form, boss style, tablets, media and sign use; they also show how re-examination can re-date an object or reveal that supposed incisions were animal marks ([Kenoyer & Meadow 2010, especially pp. 1–7](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/KenoyerMeadow%202010%20Inscribed%20Objects%20from%20Harappa.pdf)).

The same report shows why a single bag-of-strings corpus is dangerous: formal seal/tablet inscriptions, post-firing pottery graffiti, stamped vessel marks, copper/ivory/shell/metal texts and molded duplicates had different production contexts; many pottery texts are fragments; tablets can be mass duplicates while seals are mostly unique. HARP also corrected Vats's early dating of small steatite “seals,” showing they were direct-reading tablets beginning in mid-Period 3B. The [original Vats excavation report](https://www.harappa.com/content/Excavations-at-Harappa) remains valuable evidence but should not override the later stratigraphic reassessment.

**Inference.** Every analytical row should preserve: canonical object ID and source page; site/area/trench/locus/level; phase and confidence; object class/material/side; seal matrix versus ancient or modern impression; motif separately from text; observed direction; normalized direction; damage/uncertainty; duplicate/mold family; photograph provenance/licence. A “translation” that fits pooled M77 but fails within a securely dated object class is weaker than a narrower administrative interpretation that predicts those contexts.

## 3. What computation has actually established

### Directionality

**Sourced.** The usual intended orientation is predominantly right-to-left, but the physical surface matters. Yadav et al. explicitly treat a carved seal matrix and its positive sealing as mirror orientations; the 2021 artifact study reports at least 23 mirrored asymmetric sign pairs, plus opposite-direction and boustrophedon examples ([Yadav et al. 2010, Fig. 1 discussion](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0009506); [Daggumati & Revesz 2021](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-021-00713-0)). Ashraf and Sinha's terminal-sign Gini/entropy asymmetry also has the sign expected for right-to-left reading, but the database had already reversed the minority left-to-right strings into the majority orientation; it corroborates rather than blindly discovers direction ([PLOS 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0190735)).

**Inference.** Store the visible order and an intended-reading normalization separately. Never reverse every photograph by rule; determine whether it is a matrix, ancient impression, modern impression, tablet, sherd or multiline composition.

### Entropy and the writing/non-writing question

**Sourced.** Rao et al. compared bigram conditional entropy for the 1,548-line EBUDS subset with linguistic corpora and a small set of artificial/biological controls, finding the Indus value in the linguistic range and closest among their representations to Sumerian and Old Tamil ([Science 2009, DOI 10.1126/science.1170391](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391); [paper plus supplement](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/ScienceIndus.pdf)). The supplement itself notes that the Old Tamil proximity changes with script representation and says language affinity needs a more sophisticated grammatical comparison.

Sproat then tested a broader collection of real non-linguistic systems and argued that the published entropy measures did not discriminate reliably; the exchange continued in print ([Sproat 2010](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00011); [Sproat 2014](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2014.0031); [Rao et al. 2015 response](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2015.0055); [Sproat 2015 reply](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2015.0058)). Farmer, Sproat and Witzel's earlier non-linguistic/emblem thesis uses text brevity, sign proliferation and absence of long texts as central evidence ([2004 article, DOI 10.11588/ejvs.2004.2.620](https://doi.org/10.11588/ejvs.2004.2.620)). Kenoyer and Meadow explicitly treat the distinction as unresolved in the absence of multilingual, long, or successor texts.

**Inference.** Conditional entropy supports **organized sequences**, not a unique conclusion of spoken language, Proto-Dravidian, or a logosyllabary. Old Tamil being nearby on one plot is not language identification. Every proposed discriminator should be benchmarked against real administrative notations, heraldry/emblems, calendrical systems, maker marks and generated controls matched for corpus size, alphabet size, text length and positional constraints.

A particularly strong current red-team result is Ponnusamy, Jayan and
Shanmugam's *On the Non-Specificity of Statistical Measures Used in Script
Decipherment* (submitted 4 August 2026). Their purpose-built nonphonetic SIGIL
system reproduces many entropy, frequency, positional, predictive, classifier,
and network signatures used in Indus studies. Their language-key experiments
also degrade under grouped holdout and produce unstable keys. This does not
show that Indus is nonlinguistic; it shows that those statistics alone cannot
choose the encoding system or language ([arXiv:2608.02999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02999)).

### Sign ordering, local dependencies and restoration

**Sourced.** On EBUDS, 69 signs account for about 80% of occurrences and M77 sign 342 alone for roughly 10%. Eighty-two beginner signs versus 23 enders account for 80% of their respective positions. Bigram correlations produce most of the model gain; trigram gains are smaller and quadrigram/higher gains nearly saturate, unsurprising with mean text length around five. Held-out restoration is reported at about 74–75%, but the success criterion accepts a set of high-probability candidates rather than necessarily an exact top-1 reading ([Yadav et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0009506)). A first-order model also recovers start/end preferences and distributionally similar sign classes ([Rao et al. 2009, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0906237106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0906237106)). A Wells-corpus network study used a filtered set of 1,821 complete, unambiguous, single-line, deduplicated sequences (593 observed signs, length 1–13) and found a sparse directed network with less connectivity/reciprocity than within-inscription randomizations plus recursively segmentable longer texts ([Sinha et al. 2011, DOI 10.1016/j.csl.2010.05.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csl.2010.05.007); [open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4997)). Oakes independently found very strong sign-position association in Mahadevan's aggregate tables, but also found rare-sign vocabulary-growth models failed goodness-of-fit, making extrapolated total signary estimates unreliable ([Oakes 2019, DOI 10.1080/09296174.2017.1406294](https://doi.org/10.1080/09296174.2017.1406294)).

**Inference.** These are strong constraints on admissible readings: candidate values should preserve observed slot and neighbor behavior. They do not supply phonetic values or meanings; n-grams are equally capable of modeling catalog codes, formulas, protein sequences and music.

### Segmentation

**Sourced.** Yadav et al. report that 88% of texts of length five or more can be segmented using frequent signs/n-grams and complete two-to-four-sign texts; bigrams/length-two texts alone account for 52% ([paper and abstract](https://www.harappa.com/content/segmentation-indus-texts)). ICIT/Fuls provides a separate automated-segmentation and positional-analysis line of work ([ICIT publications](https://www.epigraphica.de/indus/menueindus.htm); [Wells/Fuls volume DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvr43jmf)).

**Inference.** Corpus-internal re-use shows formulaic substructure, but the discovered boundaries are not automatically words, morphemes or names. Because the segment dictionary is built from the same small corpus, validation must hold out whole sites, phases and mold/duplicate families—not random strings that leak near-duplicates into training.

### Geography and object context

**Sourced.** Distributional models can predict excavation-site relationships and classify some inscriptions by provenance ([Recchia & Louwerse 2016, DOI 10.1111/cogs.12311](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12311)). ICIT permits site, period and object-type filtering, while warning that recovery is geographically irregular.

**Inference.** Site predictability is evidence for regionalized use, not necessarily dialect or language: workshop, object-type, chronological and excavation-sampling confounds can produce the same result. Test those variables jointly.

## 4. Language hypotheses: research programs, not results

**Sourced.** Proto-Dravidian is the most extensively developed linguistic program in the Mahadevan/Parpola tradition, using rebus and structural arguments ([Parpola, “A Dravidian solution,” 2010](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/files/127256525/Parpola_A_2010._A_Dravidian_solution_to_the_Indus_script_problem.pdf); [Mahadevan's critical review of recent attempts, DOI 10.11588/ejvs.2002.1.833](https://doi.org/10.11588/ejvs.2002.1.833)). Indo-Aryan/Sanskrit, Munda, numerical/non-linguistic and other proposals also exist; the primary statistical literature states there is no consensus ([Yadav et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0009506)).

**Inference.** Proto-Dravidian deserves explicit testing, not privileged decoding. Its rivals deserve the same scoring protocol. A language-family hypothesis should be rewarded only for predictions unavailable to the model-builder: recurrent sign values across unrelated artifacts, plausible morphology that matches positional distributions, ancient rather than modern lexemes, archaeological semantics, economy of homophony/allography, and performance on sealed held-out data. Genetics, modern identity, visual resemblance, or a chain of unconstrained rebuses cannot identify the written language.

## 6. Counterexamples and failure modes a decipherment must survive

1. **Non-linguistic structured codes.** Zipf-like frequencies, entropy, positional restrictions and n-gram predictability occur outside language. Compare against real and corpus-matched artificial controls, not random-versus-rigid straw men.
2. **Text brevity and underdetermination.** Mean length is about 4.6–5; fewer than 1% of M77 texts have ten or more signs in Farmer et al.'s count, and the cleaned single-line set tops out at 14. Thousands of sign-to-word mappings can be made to fit post hoc without a bilingual or phonetic anchor.
3. **Duplicate leakage (“TAB effect”).** Molded/copy tablets and repeated sealings inflate sequences. Deduplicate by physical object and production family before train/test splitting; preserve both raw and deduplicated counts.
4. **Allograph circularity.** Merging a rare form because it improves a language fit, then citing that fit as proof of the merge, is circular. Version all merges and test visual/contextual evidence independently.
5. **Direction and surface confusion.** Seal matrix, impression and direct-reading tablet can reverse the same sequence. Multiline/boustrophedon cases defeat blanket RTL normalization.
6. **Context pooling.** Site, phase, workshop, motif, medium and function are not interchangeable. A global model may learn excavation history or object mix.
7. **Damage and editorial certainty.** Missing/ambiguous signs removed from EBUDS are not missing at random. Publish posterior alternatives instead of silently choosing one transcription.
8. **Rebus freedom and anachronism.** Unconstrained picture naming, homophones, modern vocabulary and arbitrary sign splitting make any target language fit. Fix the lexicon/time depth and scoring rules before looking at test inscriptions.
9. **Motif leakage.** Animals and field symbols may be semantically related but are not automatically sign tokens. Model iconography in a separate channel and ask whether it predicts text distributions.
10. **Rights leakage.** Publicly viewable CISI/app data is not necessarily openly licensed. A research cache and a distributable corpus are different products.

## 7. Evidence-first build order

**Inference / recommendation.**

1. Freeze a provenance-rich artifact registry from M77/RMRL IDs, CISI IDs and ICIT/Wells crosswalks. Store raw observations before normalization.
2. Reproduce published counts and n-gram/entropy results on M77/EBUDS, then rerun them by site, phase, object type, direction and deduplication policy. Any failure to reproduce is a corpus bug before it is a linguistic discovery.
3. Build a licensed image layer from public-domain excavation plates and CC0 museums; use copyrighted CISI only as a verification reference unless permission is obtained.
4. Fit a joint model with explicit latent variables for direction, allograph, formula slot, object class, site and phase. Its first outputs should be uncertainty-calibrated sign restoration, clustering and anomalous-object detection.
5. Pre-register competing semantic models—Proto-Dravidian, other linguistic families, numerical/administrative, emblematic/mixed—and score them on withheld sites/phases and newly added inscriptions. Require each to predict more than it assumes.
6. Publish negative results, alternative transcriptions and exact corpus hashes. The honest near-term product is a falsification engine and ranked set of structural hypotheses; an actual translation becomes defensible only after independent phonetic/semantic anchors emerge.
